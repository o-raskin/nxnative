## Item 4: 6K and 8K render resolutions (blocked)

- **Verified:** the render-target arena size is one 32-bit `mov` at `main+0x40e0b0` (`086ca252`, 310 MiB at 1600x900; the manifest scales it by pixel count in 64 KiB units, a MOVZ with shift 16 so the value tops out at 4 GiB); 6K needs about 4.5 GiB and 8K 7.9 GiB. The exposure reduction (two compute passes; stub `AC_LUMA_REDUCE` in `patches.py`, patch sites `0x3ffbf8`, `0x4066a0`) works on a 256-float scratch buffer and a single 256-thread final reduce: with the pyramid ending at 1/32 of the picture a 6144x3456 image yields 336 sums.
- **To do:** replace the arena `mov` with a stub that loads a 64-bit size (a rewritten sequence, build-gated), add a sixth level to the exposure chain or a two-stage final reduction (a crop of the average would be a workaround and change exposure), re-measure the GRAPHIC BUFFER growth (`0x575a30`, `0x575c94`, 8 bytes per pixel above 1080p) at 6K and 8K, and test with `DM_APP_POOL` above 10 GB. Memory on the target Mac is the practical limit. Estimate 2-3 days, complexity 4.

## Item 9: shadow range and cascades (done 2026-11-18)

- **Where the matrices come from (found with lldb hardware watchpoints on the running host: `process attach --pid`, `watchpoint set expression -w write -s 4 -- <addr>`, `register read pc lr`, `bt`; the main module's base is in the log line `main: base`):** the cascade matrix in the uniform buffer (`heap+0x17e2c700`) is written by the matrix copy `main+0x9e29d0` (a transpose store at `main+0x9b2690`), called from `main+0x406dd8`, a large constants-upload function that copies `[global 0xe98]+0x3b0` and `+0x2b0` into the buffer slots. That global is the cascade set object at `main+0xe582f0`: its `+0x3b0 + 0x40 * i` (four matrices) are the products `view * ortho` made at `main+0x420940..0x420970` by `main+0x9b2250` from `+0x2b0 + 0x40 * i` (the orthographic boxes) and `+0x1b0 + 0x40 * i`. Each box is built by the per-cascade fit `main+0x420a00(this, index)` (called in a loop at `main+0x42092c`, the count from `global 0xab8 + 0x12c`), which fits the box to the slice of the camera frustum between split distances `[x24 + 4 * index]`, where `x24 = main+0xd6faf0` is a copy of the object's `+0x14, +0x18, +0x1c, +0x20` made by the first instructions of the fit.
- **The splits are data, not constants (verified):** the live values in the first stage were 11, 30, 150 and 200 m, written by `main+0x420100` (a blend of two area records by a weight: `result = from + (to - from) * t`, reached through `main+0x41f5f0`); the constructor `main+0x420650` writes 10, 30, 150 and 300 as defaults, which are overwritten (a first patch of those four immediates changed nothing). The patch therefore sits at the copy.
- **The patch (build-gated; `patches.py` `range_words`, `runtime_patches.py` `generate_range`, variable `DM_AC_SHADOW_RANGE`, launcher row `Shadows: Range` in `settings.tsv`, custom preset only):** the 14 instructions from `main+0x420a34` (the four split copies and the call setup) become a branch to a stub that does the same copy with each value multiplied by 1.5, 2, 3 or 4 (`fmov s1,#k; ldr s0; fmul; str`) and returns to `main+0x420a6c`; s0 and s1 are free there. Verified by the matrices the host logs with `DM_AC_SHADOWADDR`: at x2 the first row's scale halves (0.104 to 0.0516), so the 5.3 m cascade becomes 10.6 m. The unit tests pass; the build was made to a scratch app (`ac_range_test.app` in the work folder), not into `/Applications`.
- **Limits:** a wider range spreads the same atlas over more area, so shadows near the camera soften by the factor unless the atlas grows (`DM_AC_SHADOW=ultra`); the margins in the light box (the constants 400 and -200 at `main+0xb5d1d8` and `0xb5d064`) and the fade of the last cascade were not examined, so at x3 and x4 a far fade or clip may show (not looked at). A frame pair at the Ultra atlas (original against x2) shows no artifact and no visible change in the night scene; the benefit would appear where shadows reach beyond the first stage's 200 m, which no tested scene has. The `Maximum` preset was not changed to use it. The installed `/Applications/Games/Astral Chain.app` was not rebuilt.

## Item 13: better screen-space GI (tried, no visible gain)

- **Current implementation (verified, shipped):** `DM_AC_GI` Low / High scales the bounce gathered by the occlusion kernel (`dm/gfx/msl/ac_ao.metal`, function `ao`): at each of the 8 / 16 / 32 AO samples inside the 1.5-unit radius, the lit radiance of the occluding pixel (capped at 2.5) times its occlusion weight is accumulated, scaled by `2*strength/count`, faded from 60 to 120 units, temporally accumulated (history blend 0.5 weighting), and added in `ao_apply_gi` with an assumed albedo of 0.35.
- **Experiment:** far-field term (full code in `gi_far_field_kernel_snippet.metal`, with the host changes listed in its header): 6 / 10 / 16 samples on rings from 1.5x the AO radius to 35 % of the picture height, reading a mip chain (`generateMipmapsForTexture` exists on `MTL4ComputeCommandEncoder`) of the lit picture copy, cosine at the receiver (minus 0.1) times `1/(1+d2/36)` times a visibility from 4 depth-tested steps along the 3D segment (each step projected, not interpolated in screen space; a first version interpolated in screen space and blocked everything). **Result (verified):** on the Highway Rescue road (1080p, in-run A/B with `DM_AC_AB=gifar`) the mean change was 0.2-1.1 of 255 levels and not brighter; at 8x strength and mip 0 (debug `DM_AC_AODBG=4/5`) the road still showed nothing. **Causes (not separated):** the road is lit by the fires' own game lights, a ground-level fire hits a ground plane at grazing angles, no albedo is available; the debug modes also replace the AO factor with the motion buffer, so they are not a clean view of the term.
- **If revisited:** try a daylight or bright-wall scene (Highway Rescue is a dark night scene dominated by emissive fire), view the term alone with a clean debug output (do not reuse `DM_AC_AODBG` 3+ which also overrides visibility), consider true radiance cascades (probe grid per 4 / 8 / 16 / 32 px with 4x directions per level, merging from the coarsest level) or reflective shadow style light injection; check first that the near-field term is visible in the scene at all.

It never produces the lit colour of an object the camera has never seen. The gain is visibility correctness: reflections no longer leak through thin foreground objects, a pillar between the road and a fire hides the fire in the reflection, and thin objects reflect; the look otherwise stays the SSR's look.

First milestone: the closest-hit position map shown as a debug image (hit distance), compared with the SSR's own hit distance, on the wet road under the truck.

Multi-bounce is a feedback: the lit colour already contains last frame's bounce, so it must be damped or it brightens and smears.
  - **Evidence against, from this work:** (1) item 13: the far-field bounce on the Highway Rescue night road changed it by 0.2-1.1 of 255 levels (not brighter) at the shipped strength and showed nothing at 8x with mip 0, and the shipped near-field bounce (`DM_AC_GI`) was measured at 0.1 away from the fires; (2) the game has no albedo (forward rendering), so a hit's lit colour is the product of the surface's albedo and light and cannot be turned into the outgoing light a bounce from that surface would give toward a different point except by assuming albedo (the shipped bounce assumes 0.35); (3) the scene is lit by the game's own point and area lights, ambient cubes and baked probes (item 7's probe, the ambient cube in slot 5 with its factor in c5 row 1), which already include a baked estimate of the bounce the artists wanted; adding a traced one on top double counts and can brighten what the art direction keeps dark; (4) the only emitters of note in the test scene are the fires and signs, whose own light reaches the road through the game's lights; (5) item 21's measurement shows that most of what a ray from the road would see is off screen or sky, where this method has no colour.
  - **What would change the verdict:** a daylight scene with large bright walls and floors (the city stages, interiors, the later districts), where indirect light from big lit surfaces is large and visible; a stage with strong coloured surfaces next to neutral ones (colour bleeding); and an albedo or material source (none exists: the game's material constants are per variant and not located). Check first with the cheap measure of item 13 (the far-field kernel, in `research_scripts/gi_far_field_kernel_snippet.metal`, which needs only a mip chain and no ray tracing) on such a scene; if that shows nothing there, ray-traced GI will not either, because visibility is the only thing ray tracing adds.
  - **Estimate if built:** 6+ weeks as listed, on top of items 19 and 21; not planned.

## Item 18: prototype result (2026-11-18)

- **What was tried:** the kernel of `shadowreceiver_lv2_velocity` (`7046415660d93f94_b`) is a Gaussian-weighted 4x4 pattern of hard compares (17 reads of the nearest-filtered atlas in two stages: the first nine taps, and the other eight only if those nine do not all agree), at offsets of plus or minus 0.5 and 1.5 times a spacing `s` around the point; the weights come from the immutable constants row `(0.99, 0.8, 0.45, 0.15)` (`c1`), the sum is divided by 8.4. `s` is read from the shader's constant buffer 13 (fragment bank 10, 240 bytes), row 2, one float per cascade (`_m0[2].x .y .z .w`). At the Ultra atlas the game's value is 0.000244141 = 1/4096 for every cascade, i.e. one tile texel (the game scales it with the atlas). `nvn_profile_uniform` (`graphics_runtime.inc`) now scales those four floats by `DM_AC_SHADOW_SOFT` for that shader only (a copy of the buffer in the arena; the game's buffer is untouched); `DM_AC_AB=soft` switches it off in turn.
- **Result:** at x3 the change is below what the test frames show (in-run A/B one frame apart: region means identical to 0.1; the difference image shows only particles, UI and faint edges near the character's feet); at x16 the character's ground shadow is not in view in the test camera, so no visible result either way. Why: at the Ultra atlas a tile texel is about 2-3 mm on the road, so a spacing of 3 texels is a penumbra of under a centimetre; a visible sun penumbra needs a spacing of tens of texels, where 17 hard taps band into 17 grey steps. The scaling therefore cannot replace a better kernel.
- **What a real fix needs (not built):** the substituted shader (route A/B of item 18): a copy of that shader's MSL with a new kernel (a rotated disc of 16-32 samples with a bilinear compare, then, for contact-hardening, a blocker search of 8-16 taps and a penumbra width from the average blocker depth, using the cascade's depth range), compiled with the host's MSL (`host_msl.py`) and selected for the key in the pipeline build, plus the same for the sibling shaders (lv3, lv4, velocity variants, spot) if they appear. ## Item 18: the substituted shadow receiver (done 2026-11-18, launcher row `Shadows: Filter`)

- **What it is:** `dm/tools/astral_shadow_receiver.py` copies the chain's translated MSL of `shadowreceiver_lv2_velocity` (`7046415660d93f94_b`) and replaces its tap block with `ac_shadow_pcss` (output: `dm/gfx/msl/ac_shadow_recv.metal`, compiled with the host's other MSL by `host_msl.py`): a 12-tap blocker search over a disc of `DM_AC_SHADOW_PEN` metres (default 0.15), the penumbra from the average blocker distance (`(zr - zb) / az * theta`, theta = `DM_AC_SHADOW_SUN` degrees, default 0.8, clamped to 2 mm .. the largest penumbra), then a 24-tap rotated disc of bilinear compares (`gather` of four atlas texels with bilinear weights), a receiver-plane depth bias from the screen derivatives (slope limited to 3) and a tolerance of 0.7 per unit distance for curved or sloped surfaces; per-pixel rotation by interleaved gradient noise. The cascade choice, matrices, motion output, entry points and function constants stay as translated. The metres are got from the shader's own constant buffer 13 (rows 7 and 9 for cascade 0, 11 and 13 for cascade 1: the lv2 variant filters only the two nearest cascades: 0-10 m and 10-30 m; `c13` row 0 holds the splits 10, 30, 150, 300): uv per metre = 0.25 * |row 7|, depth per metre = |row 9|.
- **How it is used:** `nvn_profile_shader_library` (a hook in `shader_load`, `nvn_mtl.m`) returns the host library for that key when `DM_AC_SHADOW_FILTER=pcss`; the parameters travel in c13 row 1, y and z (`nvn_profile_uniform` writes them into a copy of the buffer). Preset: Maximum only. Launcher row `Shadows: Filter` (Original / Soft contact), custom preset.
- **Verified:** the sun mask (render target ser 27, `DM_NVN_TEXDUMP`) at the same frame with and without the filter (frame 9000, 1080p, Ultra atlas): the character's cast shadow keeps its shape, the edge is sharp at the feet and widens along the shadow (contact hardening; the original's edge is nearly uniform), the stair steps of the 17 hard compares are gone; frames at 1440p with the filter ran clean (5 gameplay frames, no corruption). **Not verified / limits:** in the night test frames the final picture shows no visible difference (the sun is weak against the fires); thin casters (the sword's shadow) show per-pixel noise (24 taps, no temporal filter); self-shadowing of the character's back now follows the filter's geometry rather than the original's (the back faces away from the sun, so it is dark in both); the lv3 / lv4 / spot / particle receivers keep the original filter, and so does everything beyond 30 m; one crash in a first run with the filter (inside Metal's binary archive lookup during the title, not in the shader's draw; the same configuration ran clean twice after; cause not found, one occurrence); gather's component order was taken from the Metal specification and not checked against hardware (a wrong order shifts an edge by under one texel).
- **Cost:** not measured.

## Item 20: cost at 1440p (measured 2026-11-18)

Method: `DM_NVN_GPUPROF=every:100` (Metal 4 counter heap timestamps; the sum of all encoders of a profiled frame), 1440p, High atlas, SMAA High, occlusion Low (it runs the trace), `DM_FPS_TARGET=120`, the Highway Rescue save; medians over the profiled frames in which the casters were captured (more than 100 encoders), against the same run without `DM_AC_RTSHADOW`.

| | GPU ms per frame (median) | encoders |
|---|---|---|
| no ray-traced shadows | 4.3 to 5.1 | about 55 |
| ray-traced shadows, 16 rays per pixel (full resolution) | 19.9 | 217 |
| ray-traced shadows, 2 rays per pixel | 12.7 | about 190 |

- **The ray tracing itself is cheap:** the compute encoders (casters to world space, the acceleration structure build, the trace) took 1.9 ms at 16 rays and 0.7 ms at 2 rays on the M4 Max (so about 0.55 ms for the conversion and build, and 0.08 ms per ray per pixel at 1440p full resolution).
- **The capture is what costs:** the prototype replays every caster draw in its own render pass and so splits the shadow pass into about 50 passes, each reloading and storing the depth atlas: 7.7 to 13 ms of GPU and, on the CPU, `lc_land` + `rp_flush` + `mdi_wait` per captured draw (a wait for the GPU each time; not measured, but a stall). That is the +8 to +15 ms above; it is not the cost of ray tracing.
- **What that means for 2K:** as built, 1440p would run at about 50 to 80 fps (GPU only; the CPU stall comes on top). With a capture that runs all caster draws in one encoder beside the shadow pass (no splitting of the game's pass, no per-draw waits), the expected GPU cost is the baseline plus about 1 to 2 ms (4 rays at half resolution would be below 1 ms of trace), so 60 fps with a wide margin and 120 fps plausible at 1440p; this expectation was not measured because that capture does not exist yet. The mask is not yet applied to the picture, so the pass that would write it (small) is not in these numbers.
- **Caveat on the data:** the three runs reached the caster scene at different frame numbers (the 2-ray run only at frame 35,750), so their scenes differ somewhat (54 caster draws in the first frame, 28 afterwards); within each run the numbers were steady (p90 within 3 % of the median in the 16-ray run).

### Item 20: capture of the widest cascade only (2026-11-18, measured)

- **Why not "one encoder" yet:** a capture draw needs a render encoder of its own (no attachments), so it cannot run inside the game's shadow pass encoder; and deferring all captures to after the pass would need each draw's bound constant buffers (bone palettes, matrices) snapshotted at its draw time, which `encode_args` does not offer (it binds addresses into game memory or arena copies as of the draw's replay). That is a larger change to the replay, not done.
- **What was done instead:** the game draws every caster once per cascade, and the widest cascade contains every caster, so `acrt_capture` now keeps only the draws whose light matrix has the smallest scale (the widest box), judged by the previous frame's smallest scale with 3 % tolerance (the first frame captures all). In the test scene: 15 caster draws captured per frame, 13 left to the widest cascade (54 captured in the first frame, as before). The data is the same geometry for the trace (every caster once instead of up to three times).
- **Measured (same method as above, 1440p, 16 rays, frames with the casters captured):** GPU 10.2 ms per frame (median over 172 profiled frames; compute 1.6 ms) against 19.9 ms before and about 4.3 to 5.1 ms without ray-traced shadows: the extra cost fell from about +15 ms to about +5 ms, of which about 1.6 ms is the structure and the trace and about 3.5 ms is the 15 splits of the shadow pass. At 1440p that is a GPU-bound rate of about 100 fps for the capture plus a 16-ray full-resolution trace; with 2 to 4 rays at half resolution and a capture that does not split the pass the cost would be lower still (not built). **Not verified:** whether the geometry of the widest cascade alone matches the all-cascade set (the trace was not compared again); the CPU side of the capture (the waits in `lc_land` / `mdi_wait`).
- **Next steps for cost, in order of expected gain:** cache the static casters' world positions (6 of the 16 unique draws are `shadowcast_vs_xxxxx`; their vertex outputs change only with the camera's light matrix, which is why the capture stores clip positions: store the world positions of static draws once and skip their capture while their buffers and constants are unchanged), then the one-encoder capture above, then fewer rays at half resolution.

## Item 20: the traced sun mask (applied 2026-11-18)

- **What runs:** `ac_rt_mask_pass` (rt_shadow.inc, called from the profile's pass hook before its AO checks) remembers the pass that writes the two RGBA8 targets (motion, sun mask); at the next different pass `ac_rt_apply_mask` (1) reads this frame's camera block from `acao.cam_addr` (the game writes it before the frame replays), (2) builds the acceleration structure over the widest cascade's caster draws, (3) runs kernel `rt_sun2` (ac_rt.metal): per pixel the position and a depth-derived normal, a surface turned away from the sun (N.L below 0.02) counts as lit (the material darkens it), else `DM_AC_RT_SAMPLES` (default 4) any-hit rays in a cone of `DM_AC_RT_CONE` degrees (default 1.0) from 3 cm above the surface, temporal accumulation (blend `DM_AC_RT_BLEND`, 0.85) of the previous frame's visibility reprojected with the previous view and rejected by depth and by the game's motion buffer (as the occlusion pass does), (4) fragment `rt_mask_fs` writes a depth-aware 3x3 filter of it into the mask (the same value in the four channels). If no casters were captured this frame, the sun direction is unknown, the camera is missing or the GPU has no hardware ray tracing (Apple 9 family), the game's own mask stays. The occlusion pass is forced on (Low) because it provides the camera, the depth and the sun direction.
- **Verified (1080p, Ultra atlas, mask dump of render target ser 27 and in-run A/B of the picture, `DM_AC_AB=rtmask` one frame apart):** the traced mask shows the character's cast shadow on the road with a penumbra that grows with distance from the foot (contact hardening), the thin sword's shadow, an enemy's soft shadow in the distance, and the character's body shaded by form instead of the blotches of the first version (which came from temporal ghosting on moving surfaces; the motion gate removed them); the picture pair (game mask against traced mask) shows no artifact near the character's feet and a slightly softer shadow edge on the cracked road.
- **Cost (1440p, High atlas, occlusion Low, 4 rays, `DM_NVN_GPUPROF=every:100`, 196 frames with the casters captured):** 9.5 ms of GPU against about 4.7 to 5.1 without (compute 0.8 ms: structure, trace, filter; the rest of the extra is the capture's splits of the shadow pass), about 105 fps by GPU time at 1440p; encoders 122 against 56.
- **Not verified / open:** a scene with a strong sun (the test night scene shows the shadows mostly through the mask, little in the picture); the game's `ShadowCast` casters only (trucks, barriers and cars still cast nothing, as in the game); the receivers of lv3, lv4, spot and the particle shadows still read the atlas; characters' self-shadowing now comes from the traced mask where the screen-space receiver covers them; movies and cutscenes (no check that the casters and the sun direction exist there: the fallback keeps the game's mask when they do not); iOS (macOS only); the capture runs again on every frame (the static-caster cache is not built); two runs of the profiling script did not reach the casters' scene (automation timing), the numbers above are from the runs that did.

## RT finish (2026-11-19)

**Removed from the tree on 2026-11-19 at the owner's request (the code, the launcher rows, the presets and the switches are gone; these notes remain as the record).**

What was done, measured, and what was not. All numbers are from the Mac (Apple M4 Max), 2560x1440, the opening gameplay scene under the bridge, `DM_NVN_GPUPROF` (sum of encoder durations per frame); A/B inside one run through `DM_AC_AB=rtmask|rtao` so that the scene is the same.

**1. Capture inside the game's render pass (done).** The old capture ended the game's pass and, worse, called `mdi_wait()` (commit, then wait for the GPU) for every captured draw. Now `acrt_capture` only reads the indices on the CPU (waiting only where the geometry stage's own rule `geo_index_wait` says the index buffer may still be written by the GPU) and `ac_rt_encode`, called from `draw_` once the draw's arguments are bound, encodes the point draw with the capture pipeline (rasterization off) in the same encoder, then sets the draw's own pipeline back. The captured draw is counted only when it is encoded, so a draw that never reaches the encoder leaves nothing behind. Shadow capture: encoders per frame 122 -> 59 (56 without RT); GPU time of the effect, 403 paired 5-frame blocks of one run: median 6.23 ms with, 4.93 ms without, paired difference median +0.91 ms (earlier measurement, other scene: 19.9 -> 10.2 -> 9.5 ms). Pictures: the character's shadow in the traced mask is the same as before (checked on the mask target and the frame). Not verified: a skinned draw that uses vertex pulling (`cur_pull`) is skipped by the in-pass hook (it was not seen in this scene).

**2. Every object of the scene as a sun-shadow caster (tried, removed).** The depth pre-pass geometry (98,194 triangles, 114 draws, 9.35 MB structure) was traced as well (`DM_AC_RTSHADOW=3`, an experiment). The mask turned the whole road under the bridge black and put block-shaped self-hits on the character. The cause is the game's lighting: static geometry's shadows are baked, the dynamic sun mask is meant for what the game itself makes cast shadows, so tracing everything darkens the baked scene a second time. The mode was removed; the set of casters stays what the game draws into its shadow pass. Open: lv3/lv4 and spot-light receivers (the substituted shader of item 18 only covers the sun).

**3. Ray-traced bounce light, item 22 (gate 1 failed, stopped).** Capture cost is no longer the problem (see 1). Picture: with 16 rays, an albedo proxy (the picture's own hue, fading where the surface is bright; `DM_AC_RTGI_TINT=1`) and gain 5 the trucks and barrier are grainy where off is clean, and the warm light the fires should throw onto the trucks does not show; without the proxy it is milky and grainy as before. A denoiser (temporal plus spatial) and a way to treat baked light is the missing piece and was not built: it would also be only a more exact screen-space bounce, since a hit counts only where it is on screen. `DM_AC_RTGI` stays a diagnostic.

**4. Ray-traced ambient occlusion, item 19 (done, small difference).** `rt_ao` in `ac_rt.metal`: cosine-weighted rays (4 per half-resolution pixel and frame, grazing rays leaned toward the normal, length the occlusion radius 1.5) against the depth pre-pass structure, own temporal history gated by the motion buffer; `ac_ao.metal` reads the result in place of its screen-space samples (`ssr.w`). No grain in the frame. Cost: median 3.31 ms with, 2.14 ms without in the same run (+1.2 ms at 1440p, 1049 frames each side, capture and structure included). Difference on the ground without fire (rows from 900 down, every second pixel): between two frames of the same mode the mean difference is 0.5 of 255, between on and off 1.5, on is on average 0.8 brighter; 8 % of the pixels differ by more than 2, 1.7 % by more than 8. So the effect is real, clean and subtle; it is in the Maximum preset only.

**Not done / open:** cutscenes, movies and the pause menu with ray tracing on (the guards return to the game's own mask and the screen-space occlusion when no capture exists for the frame, but it was not looked at); 4K and 5K cost (only 1440p measured); dynamic resolution; the traced mask's shadow edge is the game's own filter at 30 m and the mask is not used by lv3/lv4/spot; the `cur_pull` case above; the static-caster cache (not needed at +0.9 ms; the capture re-runs the vertex shaders every frame).

## Item 23: ray-traced shadows from the dynamic lights (recon 2026-11-19, not started)

Idea: the fires, neon and lamps light the scene but, as far as the frames show, their light is not shadowed (debris, trucks and barriers do not block the fire's light on the road). Tracing a ray from the surface to each light and multiplying that light's term by the result would add the shadows the game lacks. This is the one ray-tracing step that adds something the baked lighting cannot already contain.

**Found in the shaders (chain output `b-b6aa8fd968b7`, `dm/tools/shader_lib.py cat <lib> <key>_b.metal`):**
- The lights are in a clustered list. `viewlightposcalc.cso` (`fe93dd1846e1b1bb`, compute) reads 32-byte records from a storage buffer (world x, y, z, range in words 0-3, four words of colour / parameters after that), transforms the position by the camera matrices and writes 48-byte records (view-space position, the rest copied, clip data) for the tile culling. The number of lights is `c0[50].z`.
- The consumers are the forward material pixel shaders: 235 of the 1,348 shader keys read the fragment storage buffers `fp_s0` (the per-tile list), `fp_s1` and `fp_s2`: 190 read all three, 32 two, 13 one (one of them is the tone map, `1ff1c2675d4b87b1`, which does not light). Each has two `do ... while` loops over the tile's lights, over `fp_s1` and over `fp_s2` (point and spot or area lights; example `04163014872103b1`, lines 461-647 and 663-811 of its translated source). `PS_LIGHTING_*` and `DeferredDirLight` are effect shaders, not these.
- The game is forward-lit; there is no light buffer or G-buffer to modify after the fact. The light term can only be changed inside these loops.

**Checked on the Mac:** a ray query in a fragment function compiles and builds a render pipeline on the M4 Max (scratch test `frt.m`), so the trace can run in the material shaders themselves.

**What it would take (author's estimate, 1-2 weeks, large uncertainty):**
1. A chain-level rewrite of the two loops in about 235 shaders (a change of `DMSH_CHAIN`, `dmsh_chain.py --header`, a full shader rebuild of the profile): per light, a ray from the fragment's world position (offset along the normal) to the light's world position, the light's term times the visibility. The light's world position has to come from the record before `viewlightposcalc` overwrites it (or from the view position and the inverse camera), and the fragment's world position from what the shader already computes.
2. An acceleration structure available before the main lit pass: the structure of the depth pre-pass geometry is built today after it (at the occlusion pass); it has to be built right after the pre-pass, and bound to the fragment stage of those draws (a new argument or table slot in the rewritten shaders and in the host's argument encoding).
3. Cost: pixels x lights per tile x one ray; unknown until measured (the fires put a few lights in each tile near them).

**Unknown and to be measured first:** how many lights a frame has and where they are (log `c0[50].z` and the first records at the lighting pass); whether the baked scene already darkens the fire's light behind occluders (then the gain is smaller); self-shadowing bias on the depth-pre-pass geometry (the sun mask needed one); the point-light shadow's softness (a light is not a point: a cone of rays, noise and the temporal accumulation the pixel shader cannot have, since it has no history; a few rays per light and a spatial filter would be needed, or the term is accepted hard).

**Gate:** if the cost with a handful of lights per pixel is over 3 ms at 1440p or the hard edges look worse than the unshadowed light, stop and record.

**Probe results (2026-11-19, `DM_AC_LIGHTS_PROBE=1`, opening gameplay scene under the bridge, frames 8300-11800):**
- `viewlightposcalc` is **not dispatched** in this scene (the compute shaders that run, by key: `averagebrightness`, `tilebasedfowardplusstencil`, the particle effect kernels, `blendcubemap`). So the light records are not made by that kernel here; the game writes them itself, and the CPU can read them (the buffers are host-readable). The first plan's step "read the record before `viewlightposcalc` overwrites it" does not apply.
- What the three buffers hold, as the material shader `cd625e0ca5c0b7a7` is drawn: `fp_s0` is 192 bytes bound, one record group of 48 bytes: the first two read as view-space position and a range (`-130.3 -6.4 8.9 12.0` and `-105.6 -10.5 29.8 8.0`), then three floats that look like a warm HDR colour (`4.3 2.9 2.8`) and a flag `-5`; `fp_s1` is 14,457,600 bytes (301,200 entries of 48 bytes) of which exactly 6,000 are not empty, and those are per-tile index lists (16-bit light indices, `0xffff` empty; indices seen: 1, 2, 3, 5), not lights; `fp_s2` is 80 bytes of what looks like one more light or the sun (`-103.5 -8.8 44.2 0.951 | 6.5 8.5 -40.9 -5 | 75 150 250 20.4 | 0 0.342 0.940 8 | ...`). The names in the shader (`fp_s0` as the first read) and what is bound do not line up with the first reading, and the bound size of `fp_s0` (4 records) is smaller than the largest index seen (5): **the layout is not decoded yet**, and the number of lights per frame is therefore not known beyond "a handful (indices up to 5)".
- What is settled: the lights are few (single digits), in view space, readable on the CPU every frame. The ray count per pixel would be small. Whether they are the fires is not shown yet; the world positions (inverse view applied) must be compared with the fires' places in the frame.

Next step if the item is taken up: decode the three buffers by reading one shader's loops line by line against a dump with a known light (a fire at a known place), then log world positions of the lights per frame.

## HDR (item 14), built 2026-11-19

**Removed from the tree on 2026-11-19 at the owner's request (the code, the launcher row, the preset key and the switches are gone; these notes remain as the record).**

**Read from the tone map's translated code (`1ff1c2675d4b87b1_b`) and its constants (`DM_AC_HDRPROBE=1`, opening scene):** exposure and the bloom (`c4[2].z` = 0.0333) go into a piecewise curve (c4 rows 4-5: top 1.0, slope 0.081352, join 4.9169, 0.6): a toe, a linear piece and, above v = 4.9169, the shoulder `1 - 0.6 * exp((4.9169 - v) * 0.081352 / 0.6)`, which approaches 1.0 only in the limit (0.93 at v = 20, 0.99 at v = 40); then the vignette (c4 row 3), a clamp to 0..1, the sRGB encode, a 16x16x16 colour-grade LUT (256x16 texture, tile = blue, u = red, v = green) and the output scale (c4 row 0, w = 1). The tone map is therefore a compression, not a clip: the clamp only bites where the shoulder is within a few levels of 1.

**What was built:** (1) `dm/tools/astral_tonemap_hdr.py` makes `dm/gfx/msl/ac_tonemap_hdr.metal` from the chain's source: the shoulder is the game's up to 0.8 of the range (v = 13), then keeps the game's slope there and rolls off toward a peak (constant buffer 4, row 6, x, which the host writes: the screen's headroom, at most 4 times white; 0 = the game's own curve), and each channel of the graded result is multiplied by the sRGB-encoded excess of its linear value over 1.0 (capped at the encoded peak), so a highlight keeps its hue and the grade; (2) `graphics_runtime.inc` (`DM_PRESENT_HDR=1`) swaps that library in for the tone map, writes the peak, and copies the tone map's R11G11B10F target after its pass into a ring of three textures that `g_present_hdr` hands to the present (cleared after three frames without a tone map pass: a movie, a menu); (3) `present.metal` `fs_hdr` adds, to the game's finished picture, the excess over 1.0 of that copy where the picture shown is that tone map (within 0.03-0.09 of the clamped copy: not under the interface or an anti-aliasing blend); (4) `front_mac.m` makes the layer `RGBA16Float`, extended sRGB, `wantsExtendedDynamicRangeContent`, on a screen whose potential headroom is above 1 (otherwise the usual layer and the profile does nothing), and follows the screen's current headroom once a second (`front_hdr_headroom`).

**Verified (M4 Max built-in screen, opening scene, 1080p):** the layer is created (headroom 1.2 at start, at most 16; the peak followed it from 2.5 to 4.0 in the log); the tone map target dumped raw: with HDR on, the largest channel is 1.81 and 1.77 % of the pixels have a channel above 1.0, where red is above 1 the mean colour is (1.37, 0.97, 0.57), an orange, so the hue and the channel mapping (red from the first input channel, green from the second, blue from the third) hold; with HDR off the largest channel is exactly 1.0 and nothing is logged, i.e. the game's own path; the window on screen is the game's picture, not black or garbled (a `screencapture` frame, which is SDR and cannot show the brightness); GPU time 7 ms at 1080p in the HDR run (Metal HUD); 69 tests pass, including the shoulder's maths (the game's curve up to the knee, continuity and slope at the knee, monotonic, below the peak).

**Not verified, to be judged by the owner on an HDR screen:** how the highlights look (brightness, whether 4 times white is too much or too little, whether the knee at 0.8 is right); `DM_PRESENT_HDR_PEAK` and the code constants are first guesses. Known limits: SMAA runs on the values above 1 (its edge detection sees brighter highlight edges); the pixels the interface or an AA blend touch show the game's picture without the extra (a thin SDR rim at the edge of a bright shape is possible); not with `DM_PRESENT_UPSCALE=metalfx`; the offscreen screenshot path stays SDR; cutscenes and movies were not looked at; 4K and 5K not measured (the extra work is one copy of the scene-sized target and one more texture read in the present).
